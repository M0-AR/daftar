Research log: best practices checked against Daftar (2026-11-09)

Rule followed: no change without (1) an external source and (2) a local measurement or a failing test that the change fixes. Rejected ideas are listed too, with the evidence against them.

Retrieval numbers come from scripts/eval_retrieval.py (27 labelled questions: an English contract, English research notes, two Arabic documents, one vocalized). Live numbers come from the local Ollama (qwen2.5:1.5b, qwen2:0.5b) on this machine.

Adopted

Considered and rejected (with evidence)

Idea (source) Why not
Spotlighting with delimiters around passages (Hines et al., arXiv 2403.14720) Measured on qwen2.5:1.5b: injection success went 0/6 → 6/6 with randomized delimiters. The paper itself advises against delimiting alone.
Datamarking (replace spaces with ^) Blocks the strong attack on gemma3:1b (0/8) but leaks ^ into answers ("^18,400^USD.^"); JSON quoting blocks it equally without corrupting output.
Sandwich defense (repeat the task after the passages) gemma3:1b strong attack: still 8/8 hijacked.
Cross-encoder reranker Ollama has no official rerank endpoint (ollama #16076); generate-based workarounds give uniform scores. Revisit when Ollama ships /api/rerank.
"Lost in the middle" edge ordering (arXiv 2410.05983) Paper reports negligible gains for small retrieval sets; Daftar sends ≤ 6 passages by default. Best passage stays first, question last.
Larger default blocks (400–512 tokens ≈ 1600–2000 chars) Eval: 1000 = 1600 = 2400 chars here, 500 slightly worse. 1000 chars (~250 tokens, 15% overlap) is inside the researched 200–512 token range and keeps prompts small for 1B models.
FTS5 porter stemmer English-only (SQLite docs); the prefix rule already handles English variants and Light10 handles Arabic.
PyMuPDF instead of pypdf Faster, but AGPL-3.0; pypdf scored F1 ≈ 0.95–0.97 on legal/financial/manual documents (arXiv 2410.09871). Kept pypdf + NFKC/de-hyphenation clean-up.

Findings with gemma3:1b (installed 2026-11-09)

  • Live test passes with a citation ("2,350 euros [1]").
  • qwen2.5:1.5b resists the same strong attack under every variant (0/6), JSON included: no regression on the fallback model.
  • Control-token stripping alone did not stop the strong attack (8/8 hijacked with or without it): the repeated plain-text order is enough. Stripping is kept as a cheap layer; JSON quoting is what stops it.
  • Ollama on this machine runs CPU-only (/usr/local/lib/ollama is empty, startup log library=cpu, total_vram 0 B): about 19 s per answer. Fix: reinstall Ollama with its CUDA libraries.

Findings with qwen3.5:2b (installed 2026-11-11)

  • Unsloth GGUF Q4_K_M (1.28 GB): sha-256 matches the download checksum; registered with RENDERER qwen3.5 / PARSER qwen3.5 on Ollama 0.40.11.
  • Answer eval (27 questions, JSON-quoted passages): 25/27 correct and 26/27 cited, vs gemma3:1b 19/27 and 19/27; plain passages 23/27 vs 21/27. It answers Arabic questions instead of replying with a bare citation.
  • Injection eval: 0/6 hijacked and 6/6 correct fee in all three variants (current/spotlight/datamark).
  • Thinking is on by default (515 hidden tokens to answer "OK"); think:false cuts that to 2 tokens in 0.04 s on the RTX 3090. Daftar sends think:false by default (Settings toggle can re-enable it).
